# Case Study: Front Range Community Microgrid Deployment (2025)

## Executive Summary
In early 2025, Lumen Grid Labs, LLC partnered with the Front Range Utility Cooperative (formalized via their letter of support 2025) to commission an advanced community microgrid serving rural cooperative members along Colorado's Front Range. Lumen Grid Labs (Golden, Colorado; founded 2019; 32 employees; 100% U.S.-owned; UEI LGLSYNTH0001) supplied power conversion and battery management systems designed to provide resilient, high-efficiency local power during distribution feeder outages.

Prior to field commissioning, system dynamics were validated in our 1,800 sq ft power-electronics lab using our 500 kW regenerative grid simulator and OPAL-RT hardware-in-the-loop rig. This deployment leverages control architectures refined under our NSF SBIR Phase I 2024 award ("Grid-forming control for weak grids", $275,000; 14% faster fault ride-through than baseline) and battery diagnostics originating from our DOE SBIR Phase I 2023 award ("Degradation-aware BMS for second-life batteries", $200,000; met all milestones). Dr. Maya Okafor (CEO) served as PI on both awards, working alongside CTO Ravi Deshmukh (power electronics).

## System Architecture and Integration
The field microgrid couples an existing community solar array with a stationary battery energy storage system. Central to the installation is the HelioLink 250, a 250 kW SiC grid-forming inverter delivering a 98.7% peak efficiency. The HelioLink 250 was selected to provide stable voltage and frequency references autonomously whenever upstream utility feeds experience power interruptions.

Elena Brandt (lead battery scientist) oversaw battery integration with our LGL-BMS3 solid-state battery management system, which provides precision cell balancing within ±2 mV and a SoC estimation error ≤ 1.5%. Field deployment and commissioning procedures were led on site by test engineer Jordan Lee in coordination with cooperative line crews and research colleagues at the Colorado School of Mines.

## Operational Outcomes and Community Benefits
Across six months of active field operation through mid-2025, the microgrid managed six separate feeder disconnection events, sustaining continuous electricity to 140 cooperative households and municipal water pumping assets. Most notably, the integrated HelioLink 250 and storage asset reduced diesel runtime by 41% across the community's critical backup infrastructure, dramatically curbing local emissions, logistics costs, and noise pollution.

Key deployment results include:
- Seamless islanding transitions under 12 milliseconds without triggering member brownouts or appliance trips.
- Robust voltage regulation within ±1.5% across severe load steps up to 180 kW.
- High availability with zero inverter thermal de-rating events during sustained 250 kW peak dispatches.
- Precision balancing maintained across all cells by the LGL-BMS3 throughout 210 charge-discharge cycles.
