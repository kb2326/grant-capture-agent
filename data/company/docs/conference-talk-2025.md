## Technical Abstract
As automotive electrification expands, decommissioned battery packs offer an economical path toward large-scale stationary energy storage. However, non-uniform cell aging, internal resistance dispersion, and capacity variance create severe integration bottlenecks. This presentation outlines commercialization-focused control methodologies developed under our DOE SBIR Phase I 2023 project, "Degradation-aware BMS for second-life batteries" ($200,000; met all milestones).

Lumen Grid Labs demonstrates the validation and benchtop deployment of our LGL-BMS3 solid-state battery management system. Conventional battery management architectures often suffer from tracking drift and thermal divergence when managing aged second-life cells with divergent degradation profiles. Utilizing real-time adaptive impedance compensation, the LGL-BMS3 achieves cell balancing within ±2 mV and limits SoC estimation error to ≤ 1.5% across heterogeneous pack groupings under variable operating conditions. The platform prevents localized cell stress, mitigating thermal degradation without forcing string de-rating.

## Experimental Validation and Hardware Results
All experimental validation was performed in our 1,800 sq ft power-electronics lab in Golden, Colorado. Pack cycling profiles were evaluated using our 500 kW regenerative grid simulator alongside an OPAL-RT hardware-in-the-loop rig to replicate dynamic grid-support load demands.
