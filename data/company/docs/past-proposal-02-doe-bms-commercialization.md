# DOE SBIR Phase I (2023): Commercialization Plan
## Degradation-aware BMS for second-life batteries

### Market opportunity

Each year, more electric-vehicle battery modules reach the end of their first life. Most of them keep a useful share of their capacity. Stationary storage for commercial buildings, small solar sites and rural utility feeders can use these modules at lower cost than new cells, if the battery management system can cope with their differences. We estimate that second-life storage is a market where integrators currently lack a controller built for mixed, aged modules. Our product, the LGL-BMS3, is designed for exactly that case.

### Customers

We identified three groups of early customers.

1. **Storage integrators** who assemble second-life packs and need a controller that raises pack life and lowers warranty risk.
2. **Electric utilities and cooperatives** that want low-cost storage on weak or remote feeders. Front Range Utility Cooperative has told us it would evaluate second-life storage once safe operation is shown.
3. **Module recyclers and resellers** who want to grade retired modules and sell them with a documented health report.

### Revenue model

We plan three revenue streams.

- **Hardware sales:** the LGL-BMS3 sold per pack controller, priced to be a small share of total pack cost.
- **Software licenses:** the degradation-aware estimator licensed per pack to integrators who already have their own hardware.
- **Grading service:** a per-module health report built from the estimator, sold to resellers and recyclers.

We expect the first revenue from pilot units sold at cost to two integrators, followed by volume pricing once the Phase II prototype is validated.

### Path to Phase II

Phase I will show whether the estimator and balancing rule work on real retired modules. If all milestones are met, Phase II will build 20 pack controllers, test them in a field setting with a utility partner, and complete the safety documentation that integrators need before purchase. The company will fund pilot units from its own earnings and will seek letters of support from integrators and from Front Range Utility Cooperative before submitting the Phase II proposal.

### Competition and protection

Established battery management suppliers focus on new cells with matched characteristics. Our protection is a pending patent on ranking retired cells by remaining usable capacity, together with the tuning data we collect in Phase I.

### Risks to commercialization

Three risks could slow adoption. First, integrators may be unwilling to take on liability for packs made from used modules, so we will provide documented test data and a clear statement of operating limits. Second, supply of retired modules is uneven, and we will work with more than one module source. Third, safety standards for second-life storage are still developing, and we will follow them closely and take part in comment periods where they apply.
